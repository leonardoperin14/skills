# Persuasive copy - the 4-sales method

A generalization of the cold-traffic ad method from "how I write ads that make
strangers buy" (Derick Carneiro, Nov 2026) to any piece of copy. For cold-traffic
ads use `cold-traffic-ad-copy`.

## Principles

1. **Persuading = presenting an idea and giving reasons why it's true.** All
   copy is a sequence of reasons.
2. **Every sale runs through 4 smaller sales**, in this order:
   - **Content** - why it's worth paying attention.
   - **Hope** - why it failed before and why it will work now (the mechanism).
   - **Value** - why THIS product is the right place, and worth more than its
     price.
   - **Impulse** - why now beats later.
3. **Your job is to find what the reader wants to hear, how, and from whom** -
   not what you think should be said.
4. **Brief before text.** The brief decides what is said; a reference
   structure decides order and rhythm.

## Step 1 - Awareness level (decides which sales are already made)

My extension to the article (which focuses on cold audiences), following the
awareness-stage logic of Breakthrough Advertising:

| Reader | Sales already made | Where to spend words |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (doesn't know you or the solution) | none | all 4, open indirectly |
| Knows the problem, not the solution | part of Content | Hope (mechanism) |
| Knows solutions, not your product | Content + part of Hope | Conditions -> USP -> Value |
| Knows the product, hasn't bought | Content + Hope | Value (anchoring) + Impulse |
| Nearly decided (retargeting, cart) | first 3 | Impulse: risk, gain, loss |

The more aware the reader, the earlier product and price can appear.

## Step 2 - Premises and target

- **Premises**: product, components/features, real proof, price/plans,
  guarantee, what they gain now, what they lose by waiting. Mark [MISSING]
  where nothing exists.
- **Target**: one market (shared desire) -> one segment (shared
  condition/identity/behavior) -> avatar (primary desire, problem, pains,
  dreams, fears, beliefs, language). Talking to everyone is talking to no one.
- **Research**: start with what already works in the niche (pages, emails,
  competitor ads, reviews) - the psychographics are already there. Put original
  research into behavior: what the avatar is reading, searching and arguing
  about right now.

## Step 3 - Brief (compact version of the 17 elements)

**Content**: topic of interest to the avatar; format/tone; concept (angle);
headline/hook from a proven structure.

**Hope**: identification (describe their situation better than they can) ->
point of contact (what they already suspect) -> problem mechanism (the real
cause that confirms the suspicion) -> function mechanism (what must happen and
why alternatives don't do it) -> solution mechanism -> conditions (why they
can't get it on their own).

**Value**: USP = the answer to the conditions; features only if they raise the
odds, make the dream tangible, reduce effort or shorten time; anchoring
against the financial, emotional and time cost of not solving it.

**Impulse**: risk reversal (guarantee as an argument), gain from acting now,
loss from waiting - only if real.

## Step 4 - Map by format

- **Landing / sales page**: hero = Content (headline + subheadline); problem
  section = identification + point of contact + problem mechanism; "why
  everything else fails" = function; solution = solution mechanism +
  conditions; product = USP + features by lever; pricing = anchoring;
  guarantee = risk reversal; FAQ = remaining objections; CTA = gain/loss.
- **VSL / sales letter**: the 4 sales in full sequence, as in an ad, with more
  proof under each reason.
- **Email sequence**: one email per sale (content/story -> mechanism ->
  product/value -> close with a real deadline).
- **Organic post (X, LinkedIn, Instagram)**: focus on Content + Hope; product
  optional or one line at the end. The mechanism is what earns saves and shares.
- **Cold outreach (email/DM)**: specific identification + point of contact + one
  reason of value + a small ask. No full pitch.
- **SaaS / B2B**: problem mechanism = why the current process (spreadsheet,
  manual checks, generic tool) fails; function = what must happen (e.g.
  continuous monitoring, alert at the right moment); conditions = why doing it
  by hand is unworkable; features by the 4 levers; anchoring = hours, revenue
  or opportunities lost per month vs. plan price; risk reversal = trial,
  guarantee, easy cancel.

## Step 5 - Writing

1. If there's a reference piece that performs (same format/market), break it
   into mini-blocks by function and use it as the skeleton.
2. Fill the blocks with the brief, in the avatar's language.
3. Cut everything that isn't a reason for one of the 4 sales.

## Reviewing existing copy

When the user brings text to review:
1. Identify the reader's awareness level.
2. Label each passage with the sale/element it performs.
3. Point out directly: missing sales, elements out of order (e.g. price before
   mechanism for a cold reader), features that answer no lever, a guarantee
   used as a badge instead of an argument, urgency with no basis.
4. Deliver the corrected version with surgical changes, preserving what
   already works.

## Compliance

- Do not invent data, testimonials, results, scarcity or deadlines.
- Do not use third-party characters, brands or IP.
- Health/supplements: no claims to treat, cure or prevent disease (FDA/DSHEA,
  FTC substantiation).
- FTC: no fake reviews or fake urgency; disclose material connections; claims
  must be truthful and substantiated.
- Lawyers: follow ABA Model Rules 7.1-7.3 and state bar advertising rules - no
  outcome guarantees, no misleading comparisons, required disclaimers; keep it
  informational and skip artificial scarcity. In practice the Impulse sale for
  a law firm is limited to an invitation to contact.
- Flag at the end anything that needs review.

## Output format

1. Awareness level and target (2-3 lines).
2. Compact brief (table: sale -> element -> reason).
3. Final copy, with section labels when it is a page.
4. Compliance notes, if any.

For simple requests ("3 headlines", "one email") do the brief mentally and
deliver the copy directly, with at most one line on the angle chosen.
