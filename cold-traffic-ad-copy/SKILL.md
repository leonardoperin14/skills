# Cold-traffic ad copy

Based on the article "how I write ads that make strangers buy" by Derick
Carneiro (Nov 2026), adapted with compliance rules. For non-ad copy (landing
pages, emails, posts, outreach) use `persuasive-copy-4-sales`.

## Core principle

A cold-traffic ad does NOT sell the product. It has to make 4 sales before the
product, and every sale is made with "reasons why."

The job is not to say what you think needs to be said. The job is to find WHAT
the avatar wants to hear, HOW they want to hear it and WHO they want to hear
it from, and put all of it in one place.

Cold audiences know they have the problem, may know a solution or two, have
tried things that failed, and are skeptical. So the ad opens indirectly
(attention -> interest -> new mechanism) and only then talks product, price
and offer. Never open with price, product or offer.

## The 4 sales and 17 elements

### Sale 1 - Content (make them want to watch to the end)
1. **Format** - why the avatar lowers their guard and doesn't suspect an ad.
2. **Topic** - why the avatar would care about the subject.
3. **Concept** - why they feel compelled to watch to the end.
4. **Visual hook** - what pulls them out of autopilot and stops the scroll.
5. **Copy hook** - the opening line that decides whether they keep watching.

### Sale 2 - Hope (they understand why they failed and that a way out exists)
6. **Identification** - why they feel understood.
7. **Point of contact** - what they ALREADY suspect is causing the problem.
8. **Unique mechanism of the problem** - the missing piece that confirms the
   suspicion and explains the real cause.
9. **Unique mechanism of the function** - the action that must happen to fix
   it, and why other solutions can't do it.
10. **Unique mechanism of the solution** - the one solution that performs that
    action, plus the proof.
11. **Conditions** - why they can't get this mechanism on their own or
    anywhere else (rare, exclusive, hard to get).

### Sale 3 - Value (this product is the right place to get it)
12. **Unique selling proposition** - why your product is the best or only
    place to get the mechanism under the right conditions. The USP is the
    direct answer to the Conditions. Never invent a differentiator.
13. **Features** - each one earns its place only if it answers one of the 4
    levers: raises the odds of the result, makes the dream scenario tangible,
    reduces effort, or shortens time (credibly). Nobody buys an ingredient or
    a spec; they buy what it does for them.
14. **Anchoring** - why NOT solving the problem costs more (money, emotion,
    time) than the price.

### Sale 4 - Impulse (buying now is the safer decision)
15. **Risk reversal** - why the risk of NOT buying is bigger than the risk of
    buying. Use the guarantee as an argument (e.g. guarantee length vs.
    realistic time to results), not as a generic badge.
16. **What they gain by buying now** - bonuses, shipping, free units, bundles.
17. **What they lose by waiting** - ONLY real reasons from the premises
    (deadline, stock, price). Never invent scarcity.

## Process (always in this order)

### Step 0 - Gather inputs
Ask for whatever is missing: product page, price and offers, guarantee,
winning ads (own or competitors'), landers, target market, language. Read any
links provided. Do not start the brief without the premises.

### Step 1 - Premises (what we already know)
Brand, product, ingredients/components, attributes (testing, certifications,
usage, brand-stated time to results, side effects/warnings), price and plans,
guarantee (and refund rate if published), what they gain by buying now, what
they lose by waiting. Mark anything unavailable as [MISSING].

### Step 2 - Target: market -> segment -> avatar

**Market** = a group sharing the same desire. List the markets the product can
serve and pick ONE:
- Brand already running: the market of its highest-traffic landers and most
  frequent ads over the last ~60 days.
- New brand/product: for each candidate market find 3+ active competitors
  (drop markets with fewer than 3); pick the one with the highest combined
  traffic growth over the last 30 days. A tie or tiny gap means either is fine.
- Tools: Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, Similarweb (the article uses
  GetHookd and TrendTrack).

**Segment** = a cluster inside the market that also shares a condition,
identity or behavior. Two ways: (1) copy the segment already working for the
brand or competitors; (2) manual research (Reddit, forums, comments) for
segments nobody is targeting.

**Avatar**, in two layers:
- *Psychographics*: primary desire, problem, secondary desires, pain points,
  dreams, fears.
- *Identity*: demographics (sex, age, country, language, ethnicity/group,
  religion, marital status, family role); behavior (competitor ads that catch
  them, videos they watch, trending topics, hobbies, news, shows, other
  conditions besides the problem); perspective (beliefs, biases, enemies,
  allies, language).

Effort rule: don't spend days on deep psychographic research. It barely
changes over the years and it already lives in the winning ads, VSLs,
advertorials and funnels in the market (from any year). Mine those. Put
original research into **behavior** - that's where the best topics, formats,
concepts and hooks come from.

Exception: in markets with no direct-response history (niche SaaS, new B2B)
there are no winners to mine, so psychographic research (reviews, threads,
interviews) matters again.

### Step 3 - Brief (fill the 17 elements)
Work in 4 blocks, always asking "what is this avatar already predisposed to
accept?"

**Mechanism**
- Point of contact: what do all ads in this segment already blame? Use it.
- Problem mechanism: connect dots they already believe; don't try to convince
  them of something brand new.
- Function mechanism: why what they tried before didn't work FOR THEM
  (segment-specific makes them feel more understood).
- Solution mechanism: the component that performs the function and fixes the
  problem at the root.

**Content**
- Topic: list several facts/subjects/situations the avatar cares about
  (searches, news, common questions) and pick one.
- Format: (a) visual - reuse the setting, character dynamic and style of ads
  already performing; (b) narrative - informational, story, testimonial etc.,
  following the reference; (c) characters and their roles.
- Concept = characters + narrative + topic. Write (a) the logline and (b) the
  angle that creates an urge to hear more.
- Copy hook: first pick a proven hook STRUCTURE (e.g. "Watch what happens to X
  when you Y"), then fill it with the avatar's own language. Produce 3-5
  variations on the same structure for testing.
- Visual hook: a visual attribute congruent with format and concept that
  breaks expectations and stops the scroll.

**Offer**
- Conditions -> USP (show the product delivers, one by one, the conditions you
  said are necessary).
- Features through the 4 levers (odds, dream, effort, time). Cut anything that
  answers none.
- Time: never promise instant results the brand doesn't promise; explain why
  it takes time. A believable timeline is what makes it believable.
- Anchoring: cost per day/use vs. the financial, emotional and time cost of
  keeping the problem.

**Close**
- Risk reversal, gain now, loss from waiting - all derived from the premises.

For each element write the reason plus a "how it would sound spoken" version
(2-5 sentences).

### Step 4 - Writing
1. Pick a reference ad that performs in the same market/format. Transcribe it.
2. Break it into **mini-blocks** by function (hook, belief break, mechanism
   reveal, product presentation, proof, offer, CTA...). That is the invisible
   structure.
3. Fill the mini-blocks with the brief.

Rule: **the brief decides WHAT is said; the reference decides only the ORDER
and RHYTHM.** Never write without a brief - skipping it and asking the model to
guess the psychology is why AI-written ads come out bad.

## Risk management
- Minimize risk where leverage is low (market, segment, mechanism, hook
  structure): reuse what's validated.
- Raise the bet where leverage is high (concept, new format, new visual,
  cultural timing).
- An ad's risk is proportional to its number of new bets. Label each ad
  **conservative** (0-1 bets), **moderate** (2) or **aggressive** (3+). In a
  batch for a scaling brand: a few conservative, many moderate, some
  aggressive.

## Compliance (mandatory before delivering)
- Do not invent numbers, studies, testimonials, refund rates, scarcity or
  deadlines. If it isn't in the premises, it doesn't go in.
- Do not use third-party characters, brands, celebrities or IP (game/movie
  characters, etc.). Propose an original equivalent with the same aesthetic.
- Health/supplements: no claims to treat, cure or prevent disease (FDA/DSHEA
  structure-function rules; FTC substantiation). Avoid misleading medical fear
  (e.g. implying a drug combination is dangerous without basis). Outside the
  US check local rules (ANVISA/CONAR in Brazil, ASA in the UK).
- Platforms: Meta prohibits asserting or implying personal attributes of the
  viewer ("Are you diabetic?") and restricts sexual-health content. Rewrite in
  third person or by situation. Check each platform's current policy.
- FTC: disclose material connections, no fake reviews or fake urgency, make
  endorsements and "results" claims truthful and typical or clearly disclosed.
- Lawyers: follow ABA Model Rules 7.1-7.3 and the state bar's advertising
  rules - no guarantees of outcomes, no misleading comparisons, required
  disclaimers, restrictions on solicitation. Skip countdown/scarcity tactics.
- Flag at the end any passage that needs legal or regulatory review.

## Output format
1. **Premises** (list, [MISSING] where applicable).
2. **Target**: market, segment, avatar (summary).
3. **Brief**: table with all 17 elements - reason + how it would sound.
4. **Concept**: logline, angle, 3-5 copy hooks, visual hook.
5. **Script**: labeled mini-blocks, with scene/visual notes for video.
6. **Risk**: label and which bets are in it.
7. **Compliance**: items to review.

Write the script in the avatar's language and slang, not the user's.
